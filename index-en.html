The Moment

Do you know the point where what always worked stops working?

A board has launched three transformation programmes in a row, and none of them has really worked.

A managing director has led successfully for fifteen years and realises that this time her repertoire does not reach.

A founder has good numbers and a good deck and still gets no term sheet.

A nineteen-year-old does not know who he is or what he could meaningfully contribute.

Four different lives. The same place.

I call it the developmental edge. At this edge, more of the same brings nothing. What helps is someone who holds the space until your own answer arrives.

The Gap

No one is responsible for the transition itself.

For strategy, there are consultants.
For implementation, there are project leads.
For the mind, there are coaches.

For the time in between, when the old answer is gone and the new one has not arrived, there is no one.

And yet that is exactly where it is decided whether an organisation keeps its best people. Whether a leadership team becomes capable of deciding again. Whether a young person finds their own direction or adopts someone else’s.

A transition cannot be managed. It needs conditions under which people find their own answers. Those are what I create.

How I work

Future intelligence emerges in generative dialogue.

Many people in my field still believe their clients need more information. In my experience, the real problem is not a lack of information.

What is needed is space to recognise what is essential for each of us, to hear the whisper of the future, and connection to turn that recognition into concrete results. For this I have developed a method with four steps. I work with it in executive coaching online, in workshops with leadership teams, and in the forests of Finland. The process stays the same. What changes is the place.

01

Letting go. Making space.

So that what is old and non-essential can go and space can open again.

In the organisation: letting go of old certainties and old knowledge. Saying out loud what no longer carries, even if it was right for years.

In the forest: making space to hear the whisper of the future. Two days without a feed and without everyone else’s opinion.

02

Connecting. Back into contact.

Because development succeeds better in relationship than alone.

In the organisation: safe spaces where difficult emotions also have room and can be worked through. Only then can the threshold to the new be crossed.

In the forest: recognising yourself by being witnessed and reflected by others. Development happens in relationship.

03

Recognising. Developing a positive vision of the future.

So that inspiration and future intelligence can emerge.

In the organisation: recognising and creating well-founded confidence. Developing new strategies together, releasing collective strengths, and building a future from them that will hold.

In the forest: recognising your own strengths and your own contribution, without measuring them against a feed.

04

Shaping. Building the next step.

So that recognition becomes concrete next steps.

In the organisation: a decision the team carries, and a development sprint that ends with a first concrete result.

Back from the forest: one concrete next step and a buddy system at your side, with which you achieve something you would not have achieved alone.

About David

Why I do this work.

I love enabling people, teams and organisations to build relationships that work. The relationship with themselves, with their team, with their market and with the world around them.

I have followed this mission for more than 25 years. It led me to work as a coach, trainer and facilitator with more than 20,000 leaders in 25 countries, from team lead to the C-suite of large corporations.

I know leadership from operational reality. And I know its price.

In my early forties I came close to collapse myself. Because I had ignored my own needs for decades, and had overlooked that the most important stakeholder relationship is the one with myself.

Where it began

At 22 I sat for four days and nights alone on a mountain called Vision Mountain in the US state of Washington. Fasting. With two questions: who am I, and what is my contribution?

What showed itself then still guides me today: the work on relationships that succeed, and the inner call to create spaces and generative dialogue that allow my clients to recognise their own vision and bring it powerfully into being.

I follow this mission in the forests of Finland. In online meetings. In the meeting rooms of companies and hotels.

Two Schools

Modern leadership science. Ancient paths to clarity.

Most providers choose one side. Structure and evidence. Or depth and presence. My work integrates both perspectives, because crossing a developmental edge successfully requires an integral approach. One side is evidence-based: more than 25 years of leadership development, strengths-based development following Gallup research, and proven, award-winning blended learning architectures for organisations.

The other side rests on the ancient practices of the wisdom traditions and is embodied in more than 30 years of my own practice: meditation, contemplative work at the level of body, emotion and language, retreats lasting several months, and my own vision quests in wild places. No borrowed theory. This particular combination of modern leadership research and the timeless practices of the wisdom traditions is the reason why developments take place in my rooms that in other rooms are not even noticed.

Organisations

I have built leadership programmes and led them myself.

Over the past 25 years I have designed and led multi-million development programmes for more than 20,000 participants. First as Senior Learning & Development Consultant at Gallup, later as Managing Director and Client Director at SYNK GROUP. During that time one of my programmes received the HR Excellence Award.

Revenue responsibility, leading international teams, handling stacked crises, decisions without sufficient information: I know the side of the table my clients sit on.

Today I work with family businesses, with growing organisations and with corporations. The questions differ in scale, but it is always about a developmental edge. Every programme is built for the specific situation. Off-the-shelf training is available elsewhere.
