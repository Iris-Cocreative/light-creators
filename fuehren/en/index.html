You’ve built a great deal on your own. Some things are hard to see from inside.

When patterns that served you well stop working, more speed won’t help, and neither will the next leadership framework. What helps is a clear view of what actually needs to develop now.

Things are working. And yet.

  • Decisions take longer than they used to.
  • The same conversations keep coming back.
  • Responsibility gets stuck in the wrong places.
  • The team has outgrown the size where everything could be sorted out in the corridor.
  • For the first time, you need a layer of leadership between you and the team.
  • You and your co-founders never spelled out your roles. Now they’re rubbing.
  • There’s friction in the leadership team.
  • Or what made you successful for years simply isn’t helping right now.

The problem is rarely a lack of knowledge. More often, something has grown that is hard to see from inside.

Development doesn’t start with a method. It starts with a clearer view.

For more than 25 years I’ve worked with people at transitions like these. I don’t lay a standard model over every situation. First we make visible what is actually going on: in the person, between people, and in the system. What comes out of it isn’t a development plan. It’s a next step that fits the situation.

  1. see
  2. understand
  3. change

MIRROR

Sometimes what you need isn’t coaching yet. It’s clarity.

You describe a specific leadership situation. What becomes visible is what keeps repeating, where the tension sits and which lever will hold. You get a written situation report, and we talk it through.

You answer five open questions, which takes about 20 minutes. I read your answers myself and write the report from them. It reaches you two to three days before our conversation. In 30 minutes we test it together, correct what doesn’t fit and settle what to do now.

What you leave with is a reasoned working hypothesis about your situation. No diagnosis, no personality test.

  • Five open questions, in writing
  • A personal situation report, written by me
  • A 30-minute conversation

EDGE

EDGE widens the view to include the people you lead with.

You gather the perspectives of selected people who experience you as a leader. This isn’t 360-degree feedback or a personality assessment. It’s a focused picture of one concrete leadership challenge, over several weeks, with a practice phase in your day-to-day work.

Leadership teams

A leadership team is more than the sum of good leaders.

Teams rarely hit their limits because competence is missing.

The harder part is what happens in between: expectations nobody states, responsibilities that compete, conflicts that look factual, decisions that keep getting stuck in the same places. Chances are all of you know this, and each of you sees a different part of it.

  • Clearer decisions
  • Conflicts that hold
  • Unambiguous responsibility
  • Trust without forced harmony

Every leadership team starts from a different place. That’s why working together begins with a briefing, not a package. You tell me where you stand, and together we work out what your team needs now.
