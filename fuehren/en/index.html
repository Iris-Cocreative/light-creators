First conversations

Two sentences I hear in almost every first conversation.

We have tried everything that used to work.
I no longer know where to start.

Neither is a competence problem. Both are transitions. The business model still carries, but the curve shows it will not carry much longer. The executive is experienced, but the experience no longer fits the situation. The team works harder and moves slower.

What happens next is usually more of the same. Another programme, another reporting line, another reorganisation. The best people burn out first. Exactly the people whose energy the next answer would have to come from.

Results

What the work gives you.

Spaces where people speak honestly with each other. Where different perspectives can surface, because everyone feels welcome bringing them. Open listening is what makes that possible, and it’s exactly what I work on with the team.

The knowledge already inside the company becomes available again. In leadership teams stuck reacting, it is there, but no one can reach it.

An honest picture of the situation. No glossing over, no blame, with the people in the room who are affected.

A team that can work through difficult emotions together and becomes capable of innovation again through constructive conflict. They can correct each other instead of avoiding — even through stacked crises.

What you end up with are decisions the leadership team genuinely carries — decisions that survive everyday work, because they get built into it. And a team that shapes things again, instead of reacting to the next disruption.

All of this succeeds through the method of creative dialogue.
